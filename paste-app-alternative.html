Alternatives

A Paste alternative that stops billing you after the first payment

Paste is the better cross-device clipboard; Mac Kit is a Mac menu bar toolkit with local clipboard history plus thirteen other tools for one $9.99 payment. Paste is also a subscription, and by year two you have paid more for clipboard history alone than Mac Kit costs for fourteen tools. Here is the trade, straight.

The short answer

Buy Paste if you copy on your Mac and paste on your iPhone or iPad. That sync is the product, Mac Kit has no equivalent, and no amount of price comparison changes that.

Buy Mac Kit if your clipboard lives on one Mac, you would rather it never left that Mac, and you are tired of a $14.99 renewal arriving every year for a feature macOS nearly ships itself.

Note what this comparison is really about: licensing model. One is rented, one is bought. Both are defensible — but only one of them stops charging you.

Where Paste is still the better buy

Paste is not the app to be dismissive about. It wins on these, and the first one is not a small thing:

  • Cross-device sync. Copy on the Mac, paste on the phone. Mac Kit does nothing like this and is not going to.
  • Unlimited history and pinboards. A deliberate organising layer over months of clipboard, not just a recent list.
  • The interface. A visual, full-width clipboard browser that is genuinely nicer to move through than a compact panel list.
  • Setapp. If you already pay for Setapp, Paste is included and this comparison is moot.
  • Intel Macs and iOS. Mac Kit is an Apple Silicon Mac app, full stop.

Where Mac Kit wins

Mac Kit’s clipboard history is a tool on a panel, not a product with a subscription attached:

  • Search, filter, pin, reuse. The parts of a clipboard manager people use every day, one shortcut away.
  • Local only. Clipboard content is the single most sensitive thing on your Mac — passwords, tokens, private messages pass through it all day. Mac Kit keeps it on the machine because there is nowhere else for it to go.
  • One payment, and it is over. $9.99 once, every 1.x update included, nothing to cancel and no renewal email.
  • Thirteen other tools. screenshots and recording, Screen Draw, colour picking, Mirror with studio light, file conversion, PDF↔Markdown, new file creation, Pomodoro, keep awake, Clean Mode, clipboard history, system monitor, Macros, and Quick Action.
  • 50+ interface languages.

What switching actually costs you

The thing you lose is sync, and it is the whole reason Paste has a subscription in the first place — cloud infrastructure costs money every month, so the app costs money every month. That is coherent pricing, not a scam. It just is not what everyone needs.

If your clipboard genuinely spans devices, pay Paste. If it does not, you are paying a cloud bill for a local feature — and Mac Kit is $9.99 once with thirteen tools stapled to it.

Questions people ask

Does Mac Kit sync clipboard history between Macs?

No. History stays on the Mac that copied it. That is a design decision, not a missing feature.

How much history does Mac Kit keep?

A searchable list of recent items you can filter and pin. It is a working clipboard, not an archive — if you need months of searchable history with pinboards, Paste is built for that.

Is Mac Kit cheaper than Paste?

Over any period longer than about eight months, yes, and the gap keeps widening. Mac Kit is $9.99 once versus $14.99 a year.

Is $9.99 really one-time?

Yes. One payment, one licence key, one Mac, no renewal date.

Try it before you decide

Mac Kit is a free 7-day trial of the whole app — no card, no account — then a one-time $9.99. If it does not replace Paste for you, you have lost nothing but a download.
