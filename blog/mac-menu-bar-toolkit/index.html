Mac menu bar toolkit: your Mac doesn’t need ten apps

September 28, 2026 · 4 min read · Mac Kit

Why we built Mac Kit: 14 everyday tools behind one menu bar icon, offline, for $9.99 once.

Count the icons at the top right of your screen. If you use a Mac for real work, there’s a good chance you’re looking at eight, ten, maybe twelve of them. A screenshot tool. A clipboard manager. Something that keeps the display awake. A Pomodoro timer you opened once last spring.

Each one made sense the day you installed it. Together, they’re a mess. And on a MacBook with a notch, some of them aren’t even visible anymore.

We built Mac Kit, a Mac menu bar toolkit, because we got tired of this. Full disclosure up front: I’m one of the people making it. This post explains what it is, why it exists, and when you should not use it.

The menu bar tax

Small utilities are the best part of the Mac. They’re also quietly expensive, and not just in money.

Every single-purpose app brings its own updater, its own license key, its own settings window, and often its own subscription and renewal email. Each one runs a background helper. Each one wants a slot in a menu bar that, on a notched MacBook, has less room than ever.

None of that is a big deal on its own. Ten of them is a tax you pay every day.

What we built: a Mac menu bar toolkit

Mac Kit is one menu bar app with 14 tools inside. Click the icon, a compact panel opens, you do the thing, you’re back to work. We held ourselves to three rules.

1. One place. The menu bar is where your cursor already goes all day. Every tool lives in the same panel, so there’s nothing to hunt for and no app switching. The goal was a panel that feels capable without turning into a dashboard.

2. Offline, no account. Every tool runs locally. No login, no cloud sync you didn’t ask for, no extra downloads per feature. Your screenshots and clipboard stay on your Mac.

3. Pay once. $9.99, one time, lifetime updates included. No subscription. We think a utility should be something you own, not something you rent.

It runs on Apple Silicon Macs and ships in 50+ languages.

Fourteen tools, four kinds of work

We grouped the tools by the kind of work they support, not by feature category.

A feature list only tells you so much. Here’s what it looks like in a normal day.

Filing a bug report. Grab an area screenshot, then use Screen draw to circle the broken button and blur the customer’s email. Done in one panel, without opening an editor.

Turning a PDF into notes. Drop a PDF on the PDF ↔ Markdown tool and get clean Markdown with its images extracted. Going the other way, turn a README into a tidy PDF for someone who doesn’t read Markdown.

Getting ready for a call. Open Mirror to check your camera and turn up the studio light. Switch on Keep awake so the display doesn’t sleep in the middle of a screen share.

Protecting a focus block. Start a 25-minute Pomodoro. The countdown sits in the menu bar, so you see it without switching windows.

The small stuff. Pull that link you copied an hour ago from Clipboard history. Pick a color from a mockup and copy it as HEX. Create a blank .md file straight from Finder. Lock the keyboard with Clean mode while you wipe it down.

The math

We added up rough prices for a popular single-purpose app in each category Mac Kit covers. It comes to about $154. Some of those are one-time purchases, some are subscriptions that charge you again next year.

To be fair, nobody buys all nine on day one. People collect them over a few years. That’s exactly how the menu bar fills up without anyone deciding it should.

Where a specialist app still wins

Mac Kit is built for breadth. A dedicated app built around one job for years will usually go deeper in that job.

If screenshots are your core work (you publish tutorials, you need cloud share links and heavy annotation every day), keep your dedicated screenshot app. Same if you read detailed system graphs for a living. Mac Kit is not trying to replace that.

Mac Kit is for everyone else: people who use these tools a few times a day, want them to be fast and reliable, and would rather not manage ten apps to get there. You can also mix. Use Mac Kit for twelve jobs and keep the one specialist you truly depend on.
